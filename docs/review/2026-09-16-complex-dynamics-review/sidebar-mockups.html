What shape should the sidebar be?

Four options, each a working replica at the app's real sidebar width of 346 px, carrying the app's real groups and control labels. Open the groups, switch the tabs, type in the filter. Each frame measures its own content live and reports it as a share of today's, so the four are compared on one measurement taken four ways.

The replica's controls are a little tighter than the app's, so its absolute pixels run below the app's own 2,091 px. The ratio is what transfers — which is why the frames report a share of today rather than a raw height.

What the measurement changed

I expected the grouped accordion to take a third off the column. It takes 6 %. All fifteen groups are still in the scroll, so reordering them and moving the citation to the footer changes where things are, not how much there is. Only hiding shortens the column, and tabs are the only option that hides: they cut it to roughly a third.

So height is not the argument for B, and pretending otherwise would pick the wrong option. The real question is narrower and worth stating plainly: is the column long because it is badly ordered, or because there is simply a lot in it? The measurement says the second. B answers the first problem and C answers it differently; A is the only one that answers the second — at the price below.

The price A pays

Anti-aliasing, refine-while-idle, perturbation, relief lighting, auto-iterations and the colouring mode all silently change what the renderer draws. Perturbation is the sharpest case: with it on, the kernel renders every colouring mode as discrete escape time, so a reader who picked “Orbit trap” gets something else and the menu still says orbit trap.

Switch option A to its Instruments tab and watch the warning leave the pane. Nothing on screen then says the picture is degraded. In B, C and D that warning is in the same scroll as everything else — it can be scrolled past, but it is never in another mode. This is report finding S4, and tabs are the one option that makes it worse.

A can be rescued — tick “active-settings strip” in option A's frame and the warning returns as a pinned bar. That is the real shape of A: five tabs plus a persistent strip listing every non-default setting. It is more surface to build and more surface to maintain, and it exists to undo a problem the other three options never create.

Recommendation

B, with D as its first commit — but on narrower grounds than I first gave. D is a strict subset of B (the inspector's action inputs move into the report they act on; the citation moves to the footer), so it ships on its own in about two hours and nothing is wasted if you then want the full grouping.

I recommend B because a 2,000 px scroll that is ordered is a different object from one that is not, and because every setting stays where a glance can find it. I am no longer recommending it on height: it does not deliver height, and this page says so above rather than quietly dropping the claim.

B's five sections are exactly where A's tab boundaries would fall, so B is the step before A rather than a detour — the grouping is already done and the only remaining change is whether the sections hide. Choose A instead if the 2.3-screen scroll is the thing that actually bothers you in daily use: it is the only option that fixes that, it is a defensible call, and the conditions are that S4's visible-reason work (WP8) ships first and that A carries the active-settings strip.

Costs: D ≈ 2 h · C ≈ half a day · B ≈ 1 day · A ≈ 2 days plus the strip.
